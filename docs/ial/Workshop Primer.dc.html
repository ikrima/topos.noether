What to know before you sit down at the microscope

Read the four notes below, then take the self-check. Take it again after the session — it stores both attempts and shows you what moved.

Four things the session assumes
01

The image is made of diffracted light, not of the specimen

Light that passes through your specimen is bent into a diffraction pattern, and that pattern lands in the objective's back focal plane. The image you eventually see is what happens when those diffracted beams interfere again. Whatever fails to make it through the objective is simply not in the image, and nothing downstream can put it back. Abbe worked this out in 1873, and it is the reason numerical aperture matters more than any other number on the instrument.

02

Resolution comes from NA and wavelength. Magnification is separate.

The smallest distance you can resolve is roughly 0.61λ/NA — around 240 nm for green light and a good oil objective. Magnification beyond about 1000×NA adds size without adding detail, which is why it is called empty. A 60×/1.40 outperforms a 100×/1.25, and if you choose lenses by the number printed on the barrel you will eventually buy the wrong one.

03

Contrast methods manufacture contrast, and each leaves fingerprints

Most living cells are nearly transparent; they shift the phase of light without absorbing much of it. Phase contrast, DIC, darkfield and Hoffman modulation each convert that invisible phase information into visible intensity — by different tricks, with different artifacts. The phase-contrast halo and DIC's three-dimensional relief are both products of the optics. Neither is a structure you can measure.

04

The detector is part of the experiment

A camera samples the optical image onto a grid. Sample too coarsely and fine detail is not merely lost — it folds down into coarse patterns that were never in the specimen. Sample too finely and you bleach a live cell for no extra information. Let a pixel saturate and every intensity ratio you compute from that image is void, with nothing in the file to warn you.

Every one of these has the same shape: a setting you control, an effect you can see, and a consequence you cannot see. The session is about the third column.
