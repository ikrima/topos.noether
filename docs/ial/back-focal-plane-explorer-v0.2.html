What you are looking at when you pull the eyepiece

Brightfield, darkfield, phase contrast and DIC are not four techniques. They are one mechanism, operating at one place — the back focal plane of the objective — with four different things put in the way. Change the mode below and watch only the middle panel change.

How to use this at the microscope

Remove one eyepiece and look down the tube, or swing in the Bertrand lens. What you see is the middle panel: the objective's back focal plane, lit by the diffraction pattern your specimen is making. It is the only place in the microscope where the image has not yet formed and every spatial frequency is still laid out separately where you can point at it.

The four photographs above the dials were taken down the tube of a real microscope and are reproduced from the workshop slides. They are what the middle panel actually looks like through glass: a bright ring, which is the condenser annulus, sitting on a darker ring, which is the phase plate etched into the back of the objective. Compare them with the simulation before you go to the bench, so that when you look through the eyepiece you already know what you are seeing.

Start in Brightfield with a grating specimen and drag Objective NA down. The outer diffraction orders slide past the rim of the pupil and go dim red, and the bars in the image get softer. At the moment the first order is excluded the image collapses to an even grey — not a faint pattern, nothing at all. That is Abbe's 1873 argument, and it is why he insisted resolution is a property of NA rather than of magnification.

Then open the Condenser NA. Each direction of illumination puts the whole diffraction pattern down in a slightly different place on the pupil, so orders that missed the rim from straight-on can be caught from an angle. Open the condenser to match the objective and the finest resolvable spacing halves, from λ/NA to λ/2NA. Every textbook states that ratio; here it emerges from the model rather than being asserted.

Switch to Darkfield and notice that nothing was added to the pupil. The only change is a condenser NA larger than the objective NA, so the undeviated beam misses the aperture entirely and only scattered light is collected. The image is a map of what scatters, not of what absorbs.

Switch to Phase contrast with an unstained specimen. Look at it in brightfield first: it is nearly invisible, because it changes the phase of light but not its amplitude, and a detector records only amplitude. The phase plate does two separable things. Drag transmission down and the background dims so the diffracted light is no longer swamped. Drag retardation across zero and the contrast inverts. Zernike's Nobel is that one annulus of etched glass.

Finally DIC. The pupil filter is now a sine along the shear axis, so the image responds to the gradient of optical path rather than its value. The relief runs in one direction only. Rotate the specimen in your head and the shadows do not follow, because they are not shadows — which is the concrete reason DIC relief is optical rather than geometric, and why you cannot read height off a DIC image.

What this model does and does not do

The engine is a scalar diffraction calculation. The specimen field is Fourier transformed once; then for each of up to nineteen points across the condenser aperture the pupil is shifted, a filter applied, and the result inverse transformed, with intensities summed. That summation is what makes it partially rather than fully coherent, and it is why the λ/2NA limit appears on its own. The image builds up over a few frames as the cone is summed.

Each mode differs only in the illumination aperture and one pupil filter — in the source they are four short expressions in the same function, which is the whole claim of the piece. The specimen is built band-limited, so a spacing finer than the cutoff produces a genuinely blank field rather than a faint spurious pattern.

What it leaves out: polarisation, so the DIC Wollaston shear is folded into an equivalent pupil filter; aberration of every kind, so this is a perfect lens; vector diffraction, which matters above about NA 1.0; and any third dimension, so there is no depth of field and no point spread function along z. It is a key for reading the instrument, not a replacement for it.
