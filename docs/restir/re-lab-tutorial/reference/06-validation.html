validation/ — the harness

The chain of trust and every statistic hanging off it.

Source: docs/architecture/reference/06-validation.mdinformation-oriented

A top-level package alongside pt, not under it, because it is a peer of the renderer rather than part of it. ~2,204 lines.

The framing. Low variance is not correctness: a biased ReSTIR produces a clean, beautiful, wrong image. Changes to the estimator are argued with numbers, never with renders.

The chain of trust#

closed form  ──►  uniform MC (1 bounce)  ──►  RIS  ──►  ReSTIR DI
    │                    │
    │                    └──►  uniform MC (2 bounce)  ──►  ReSTIR GI
    │                              ▲
    └── quadrature, different parameterization ──┘

Each link is checked against the one to its left. di_uniform is the root because it is the simplest, most obviously unbiased estimator here, and it is validated against a closed form.

Why the per-sample twin diff is not enough. It is the strongest test in the project and it has a blind spot worth naming: the two implementations are supposed to compute the same thing, so a wrong pdf, a double-converted Jacobian or a doubled cosine makes them agree exactly as well as being right does. Closed form covers that gap for DI. One bounce further there is no closed form, so the indirect term is anchored against a deterministic quadrature in a different parameterization, which never forms a pdf at all and so cannot share the mistake. Measured: MC and quadrature agree to 1.6% or better at three shading points, one on a sphere.

References — reference_cache.py#

CACHE_VERSION = 1
cache_dir() -> str                                  # $PT_REF_CACHE or .ref_cache
get_converged_reference(scene, spp=4096, seed=None, force=False, on_progress=None)
cached_reference(scene, seed=0)                     # never renders; for the UI thread
reference_noise_floor(scene, spp=4096, seed_a=0, seed_b=99991) -> float
estimator_noise(scene, spp, seed_a=0, seed_b=99991) -> float    # == 2 * variance
clear_cache() -> int;  cache_report()

Contract — strategy is deliberately absent from the fingerprint. _fingerprint hashes the camera basis, prims, materials, lights, seed, spp, max_bounces and "estimator": "uniform-mc-converged". Asking for "the reference for a ReSTIR scene" therefore structurally returns the uniform-MC image — you cannot validate an estimator against itself. max_bounces is present because a two-bounce scene's reference is two-bounce uniform MC.

Contract — the noise floor is not optional. reference_noise_floor is the MSE between two independent references. Without it a detector either cries wolf on the reference's own noise or compares against zero and flags everything. Bias below this is undetectable and must not be reported.

The bias detector — bias.py#

bias_report(converged_img, reference, ref_noise_floor, estimator_noise=0.0,
            alpha=0.01, margin=2.0, decorrelation_stride=1,
            min_sign_samples=30) -> BiasReport

Three tests, because each has a blind spot alone:

TestStatisticBlind spot it covers / has
Excess over expected noiseexpected = (estimator_noise + ref_noise_floor)/2; biased if excess > margin·expectedThe only one that stays valid under correlation, since E[MSE] is an average
Per-pixel sign testtwo-sided binomial on mean-luminance error, subsampled [::stride]Assumes independent pixels, which reuse violates
Localizationper_pixel_sq > floor + 4·std → hotspot maskDiagnostic only; reuse bias concentrates at discontinuities

Contract — E[MSE(X, Y)] = vx + vy + bias². Comparing against the reference's noise floor alone charges the estimator's own variance to bias: uniform MC at 400 spp against a uniform reference at 4000 spp reports as badly biased, though it is the reference's estimator.

Contract — the sign test needs decorrelation and reports its own power. ReSTIR's neighbouring pixels literally share samples, so a per-pixel binomial test is wildly over-confident and flags every configuration including provably unbiased ones. Subsampling beyond the reuse radius fixes it, but too large a stride leaves no power — a 32px radius leaves 9 usable pixels at 96×96 — so sign_test_n and sign_test_usable are always reported, and the test is skipped below MIN_SIGN_SAMPLES = 30.

BiasReport is a dict with .biased and .summary(); 17 keys including verdict, mse, expected_noise, excess_ratio, sign_test_{p,n,usable}, directional, biased_pixel_fraction, bias_hotspot_mask.

Efficiency — convergence.py#

mse(a, b);  rmse(a, b)
convergence_curve(scene, checkpoints, reference, engine=None, on_progress=None)
estimate_slope(curve, drop_floor_ratio=None)
slope_verdict(curve, noise_floor=0.0, flat_threshold=-0.3, tail_fraction=0.5, floor_margin=2.0)
equal_time_comparison(scenes: dict, reference, budget_seconds=5.0, warmup_samples=1)
frame_variance(scene, warmup=0, trials=8, pct=90.0, engine=None, ...) -> float

Contract — slope_verdict fits the tail, not the whole curve. An unbiased estimator keeps falling near -1; a biased one flattens onto its bias² plateau. It needs only the curve to have turned over, so it fires earlier than the excess test. Fitting the whole curve dilutes the evidence, because early checkpoints fall steeply either way.

Contract — variance is measured per frame, at the 90th percentile. Which statistic you choose decides whether you see the effect at all: the mean per-pixel variance is firefly-dominated and moves under 5% across estimators; the median samples pixels that were never noisy, where RIS genuinely loses. At p90 — where perceptible noise lives — the ordering is clean: RIS 1.9× better than uniform, ReSTIR 1.5× better than RIS.

The indirect term — gi.py#

The most consequential module in the harness, because whole-image comparison cannot see GI defects at all.

SHARED_DIRECT_TERM = (int(Strategy.UNIFORM), int(Strategy.RESTIR_GI))
require_shared_direct_term(scene) -> None
one_bounce_image(scene, spp, seed=None) -> np.ndarray
render_indirect(scene, spp, seed=None, engine=None, warmup=0, on_progress=None)
converged_indirect_reference(scene, spp=4096, seed=0, on_progress=None)
indirect_noise_floor(scene, spp=4096, seed_a=0, seed_b=99991) -> float
indirect_estimator_noise(scene, spp, ...) -> float
indirect_verdict(scene, spp, reference, noise_floor, total_reference=None, ...) -> dict
indirect_convergence_curve(scene, checkpoints, reference, seed=None, engine=None)
indirect_fraction(scene, spp=4096, seed=0) -> float
energy_ratio(img, reference) -> float

Contract — differencing is exact, not statistical, and only for two strategies. SHARED_DIRECT_TERM is (UNIFORM, RESTIR_GI) — the only strategies whose vertex-0 draws are uniform MC's. For those, vertex 0 keeps its RNG stream, so subtracting the one-bounce image cancels the direct term rather than averaging it away. require_shared_direct_term refuses anything else, and also refuses max_bounces < 1. Do not roll the subtraction by hand.

Why it is needed: two bounces are only ~13% brighter than one on the Cornell box, so a completely broken indirect term still lands inside the 2%-of-pixels tolerance a whole-image comparison can afford.

Contract — warm-up is not optional for a reuse estimator. A ReSTIR run becomes the estimator under test over roughly m_cap frames, so an accumulation started at frame 0 averages in the path tracer it began as. Measured on cornell_restir_gi at 96px, warm-up 0/8/24/64/128/256 → mis 1.0037/0.9966/0.9919/0.9873/0.9872/0.9870. From cold it reads 1.004 — above truth, and indistinguishable from the uniform control at 1.0009 ± 0.0004. The reading settles by 64, which is the --warmup default. render_indirect implements the discard with reset_accumulation(), which clears the image while keeping reservoirs.

The control that keeps the discard honest is the no-reuse row measured with the same warm-up: both passes off at M=1 is the two-bounce path tracer sample for sample, and it must read 1.000 (measured 1.0004 ± 0.0027).

Contract — use the indirect noise floor, not the whole-image one. The direct term cancels stream-for-stream and takes its variance with it: 1.4e-06 vs 5.8e-03 on cornell_restir_gi at 48px. Scoring a differenced image against the whole-image floor would call every GI estimator clean.

Contract — energy resolves what MSE cannot. On the same runs, total energy reads 0.9803/0.9692 where the indirect term reads 0.8984/0.8386, and the MSE-excess verdict returns "unbiased (within noise)" on every row, because bias² on 11–18% of an image never clears both estimators' variance at a few hundred spp. energy_ratio is "the statistic that found the bugs", and it is a whole-frame mean — per-pixel probes are heavy-tailed enough to show a fake 20% deficit on a DI control.

Temporal — temporal.py#

dollied_camera(cam, factor, pivot_distance);  focus_distance(scene)
oscillating_path(cam_a, cam_b, frames)
path_frames(engine, cameras, measure_stride=2, warmup=DEFAULT_WARMUP, ...)
temporal_bias_report(scene, frames=400, magnify=DEFAULT_MAGNIFY, ...)

Contract — a moving camera has no single reference, unless it comes back. Per-pose references would cost the harness multiplied by the frame count. Alternating two poses and measuring only pose-A frames gives one reference serving every measured frame, each of whose history came from pose B. Accumulating across the path is invalid — the mean of images from different viewpoints is not an image of anything — so frames are extracted by differencing the accumulator (Engine.render_sample_radiance).

Finding worth knowing: reprojection bias comes from magnification, not from reprojection error. A correctly reprojected static point lands back where it started. The residual is pixel quantization measured in the previous frame's footprint, so it is large only when the surface was smaller on screen then.

FLIP — flip.py#

flip(reference, test, ppd=DEFAULT_PPD) -> (H, W)
hdr_flip(reference, test, ppd=DEFAULT_PPD, tone_mapper="aces", ...)
ppd_from_viewing_conditions(distance_m=0.7, width_m=0.7, resolution_x=3840)
flip_summary(error) -> dict;  magma(error) -> (H, W, 3)

Pure numpy, no external dependency; the magma table is inlined from matplotlib at 32 stops specifically to avoid one.

Contract — FLIP ranks and localizes; it does not detect bias. A converged biased estimator is perceptually smooth and scores well. Reach for it when the question is "which of these looks closest" or "where do these differ", never instead of a verdict.

Contract — the anchoring caveat, carried verbatim. Implemented from the paper, it read 7% high on every achromatic difference; running NVlabs' flip-evaluator once in a throwaway environment found it. The bug was cmax: the natural sRGB-gamut derivation (green→blue HyAB) gives 308.12, the reference behaves as 203.29, and the value is now measured rather than derived. The achromatic path is now exact; a structural chromatic residual remains, worth about +0.01 mean FLIP here with estimator ordering preserved. tests/test_flip.py pins the exact half to 1e-4 and the chromatic half to the measured gap, so either improving or worsening the agreement fails a test rather than silently changing what the column means.

These scores rank estimators here. They are not quotable against a published FLIP number.
