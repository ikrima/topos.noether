Conformance vectors

The frozen golden values a port must reproduce exactly, and why the RNG comes first.

Source: docs/architecture/conformance/README.mdinformation-oriented

Why the RNG comes first#

porting-to-racket.md step 1 is the RNG, and it is not reorderable: every later verification in that document is a per-sample diff, and a per-sample diff requires identical draws. Until a port reproduces these values it cannot be checked against anything except statistics — and statistics on ~11% of an image will not see a defect losing a sixth of the indirect term.

What is covered — the RNG#

rng_vectors.json — 2,240 vectors, ~97 KB, plain JSON so it needs no Python to consume.

SectionContents
hash4[pixel, sample, bounce, dim, seed, expected_u32]
rand1the uniform extraction, positionally parallel to hash4
layoutbounce ids, strides, and every *_dim accessor tabulated

The sweep is chosen for coverage, not volume: each axis at its extremes, a power-of-two edge, one interior value, and every BOUNCE_* id. A port that reproduces the boundaries and fails in the interior is not a failure mode a pure integer hash has.

verify_conformance.py additionally checks two things the table cannot express:

  • Injectivity over runtime-scaled blocks. No two draws may share (bounce, dim). Candidate and tap blocks are M-wide, so a fixed offset can collide for some M and not others — checked over M ∈ (1, 2, 8, 32).
  • The two deliberate invariants. gi_cand_dim(0, U/V) == bsdf_dim(0, U/V), the alias that makes ReSTIR GI at M=1 with reuse off draw the same numbers as the two-bounce uniform path tracer, so the two are comparable per sample; and shade_bounce(0) == BOUNCE_SHADE, without which differencing against a one-bounce render stops being exact and every GI measurement silently changes meaning.

coverage_scene.edn is a fixture scene rather than a shipped one, because the nine scenes in pt/scenes/ are chosen to make estimators differ and that leaves four branches of pt/core/ with no coverage at all: a sphere area light, a two-sided light, a plane as a shadow caster, and a quad with non-perpendicular edges. It lives here so adding it does not change scene_ir.json or pt edn's listing; both implementations load it by path.

What is covered — the reuse algebra#

reservoir.json — ~950 KB over five configurations, in the order a defect propagates:

SectionContentsWhat only it can catch
absorbChao's rule on integer payloads, no scene involveda streaming bug masquerading as a p̂ bug; the two rejection cases, where a non-positive or NaN weight adds confidence but keeps the incumbent
finalizefinalize_ris and finalize_mis tabulated side by sideconfusing them — the difference is one factor of M, and it darkens the image by exactly that
gatesneighbor_offsets, accept_neighbor, accept_reprojection, over taps and partners across the framereusing the spatial gate for reprojection. On taps alone the two agree trivially; only distant partners on the same plane separate them
misall three rules, Σ mᵢ(y) at a fixed y, and Σ mᵢ(yᵢ) recorded as not summing to onea port "fixing" the second into a partition of unity, which breaks the estimator to satisfy a requirement that never existed
di_risinitial RIS per pixel per samplethe resampling weight w = p̂ / p_source
framesthe three-pass loop per pixel per frame, both reservoir buffers, plus a whole-frame checksuma temporal bug and a spatial bug produce the same wrong pixel; the buffers tell them apart, and the checksum keeps the strided pixel dump honest

bias_demo appears three times, once per weighting, because it is the scene built to make the 1/M rule visibly wrong: if a port's three modes agree there, its MIS weighting is not doing anything. Measured first-frame energy at 24×24 — naive 911.1, mis 957.6, pairwise 956.7.

Two things this fixture deliberately does not pin. It never runs initial RIS at a pixel whose primary ray missed: the renderer does not, and doing so reaches materials[-1], which Python resolves to the last material and a conforming host rejects — pinning that would freeze an accident. And the property Σᵢ mᵢ(y) = 1 is not a table at all; it is quantified over configurations, so it lives in port/racket/test/mis-properties.rkt under random generation. That is what turned up PAIRWISE_MIS's conditional normalization, which no fixture generated from a scene could have shown.

What is covered — reconnection#

reconnect.json — ~730 KB over two GI scenes. The smallest section matters most:

SectionContentsWhat only it can catch
jacobian_lawshand-built inputs touching no scene, carrying the hand-derived answer and the Python'san inverted Jacobian. Reciprocity J(a→b)·J(b→a) = 1 and the identity case both hold for the inverse, so only J = 4, not 1/4 pins the direction
reconnectevery (traced-from, evaluated-at) pixel pair, including same-pixela cosine taken at x1 instead of x2; a grazing epsilon that breaks the identity shift first, silently discarding the sample the pixel actually traced
candidatestrace_path_candidate and the initial path reservoirthe secondary NEE drawn from the wrong RNG stream
gi_mergecombine_streams under GI_DOMAIN, streams built at different shading pointsthe Jacobian hook itself — direct lighting's is identically 1, so nothing in step 4 can show it doing work
gi_uniformthe two-bounce path tracer per pixel per samplethe throughput chain, and the bounce ray's own epsilon
identityboth estimators' columns, plus the residual and its eps/d bounda measure convention that is self-consistent and wrong
framesthe GI loop, both buffers, plus a whole-frame checksumfinding 23 — GI rolls the pre-spatial reservoir, DI the post-spatial one

The identity is not bit-exact, and the fixture records why rather than tolerating it. The bounce ray leaves from x1 + eps·n, so the path tracer evaluates the integrand at the direction it sampled while reuse re-derives the direction from the stored vertex; the two differ by about eps / d. At 20×20 on cornell_restir_gi, 103 of 185 samples agree bit for bit, one exceeds 1e-3, the worst is 7.3e-3, and the totals agree to 2.7e-5. The port owes something stronger than the identity — both columns exactly — with the residual's distribution checked on top, because that distribution is what moves if scatter_epsilon, reconnect or the measure convention is wrong.

What is covered — the render graph#

graph.json — ~120 KB over thirteen configurations plus five synthetic graphs. No floating-point arithmetic here, so the acceptance is different in kind:

SectionContentsWhat only it can catch
rawresources with their SSA versions, passes with reads/writes/params/domainsa builder bug, told apart from a compiler bug
plansteps, lifetimes, slots, edges, culled, and each pass's allowed resource setthe boundary contract's scope — that the guard is not merely present
plan.describethe schedule and slot table, verbatimdeclaration order, the toposort tie-break, culling, lifetimes and first-fit colouring, all at once
dirtydirty_steps over all 32 subsets, plus progressive_stepsa non-transitive propagation; the progressive schedule keyed on the wrong domain
rejectedfour configurations refused at declare time:ris silently rendering one bounce and calling it GI
syntheticfive hand-built graphssee below
malformeddouble write, orphan transient read, cyclethe three stage-A/B failure modes

Measured: two and a half of the five compile stages are dead code on every shipped scene. Nothing is culled anywhere; n_physical equals the resource count in all thirteen, so nothing aliases; and the topological sort is the identity, because assemble_di_graph declares passes in dependency order already. A port can get all three wrong and reproduce every real plan exactly. The synthetic graphs reach them:

graphreaches
dead_passstage C — a pass whose only output nobody reads
aliasingstage D's interval colouring, n_physical < resources
two_free_slotsfirst-fit vs last-fit, which agree unless two slots are free at once
diamondstage B's tie-break, declared sink-first so the sort must reorder
persistent_historythe v0 read exemption, isolated from ReSTIR

two_free_slots exists because swapping first-fit for last-fit changed nothing on aliasing — with one free slot in flight every allocation rule picks the same one. The generator asserts that culling and aliasing stay dead on the real scenes, so if the builder ever changes, the synthetic graphs' premise fails loudly rather than quietly becoming redundant.

What is covered — the frame loop#

frameloop.json — ~310 KB. Everything that only exists across frames: the state carried from one to the next, the camera that may have moved while it was carried, and the discipline that has to surround measuring an estimator which is not yet itself.

SectionContentsWhat only it can catch
camera_pathfocus_distance, the dolly, the A/B path's posesa path bug that would otherwise surface as a broken reprojection gate
reprojectionthe per-pixel history map per frame, computed from the gates directly rather than read out of the loopa loop that computes the right map and then reuses res_prev[i] anyway
framesper-frame, per-pixel radiance along the path, both payloadsthe per-sample diff, with the camera moving under it
no_reprojectthe identical path with :reproject falseseparates "reprojection is wrong" from "the camera moved"
warmup_rewindsample-0 hit points, cold frame 0, warmed frame 0, and their differencethat the warm-up rewinds the sample counter rather than skipping frames
estimate_pixelsthe cheap subset paththe refusal that exists — and the one that does not
energythe warmed indirect energy table, 9 rowsthe estimator's behaviour, which no per-pixel diff states

The warm-up rewind is the part with no signature in an image. Discarding frames is not the same as Engine.render(warmup) + reset_accumulation(): the latter also sets spp_index back to 0, so the measured frames draw uniforms 0 … spp-1 however long the warm-up was, and the subtrahend one_bounce_image renders exactly those samples. That is what makes the ~89% of the image which is direct lighting cancel per sample instead of averaging away. Skip frames without the rewind and the cancellation is lost, which reads as a few tenths of a percent of noise on precisely the statistic the deficit is a few percent of. Because nothing about a rendered image says which happened, the section pins it with a two-part claim: a warmed run's first measured frame traces the same primary rays as a cold frame 0, and produces different radiance.

The energy table is checked to the last place, not statistically. Every row is deterministic given the seed, so two implementations drawing the same numbers owe the same mean — step 1's dividend applied to a summary statistic. What is not claimed is that the digits match semantics.md §10: those were measured on the f32 kernels at 128px against a 4096-spp reference, and these are the f64 reference path at 48px. The shape is the claim, and it reproduces — no-reuse sits on the uniform control, temporal-only is weighting-insensitive to nine decimals, and MIS recovers part of what 1/M loses spatially and only part.

And the parameter that makes reprojection measurable is resolution-dependent. validation/temporal.DEFAULT_MAGNIFY is 20, tuned for 128px. At the fixture's 16px it collapses the previous frame's view of the scene below a single pixel: every shading point projects into the same sub-pixel blob or off the frame, the map comes back all -1, and the section passes against a port with no reprojection at all. Measured — 0 of 1,536 pixel-frames accepted, on both scenes. The fixture uses 3.0, chosen by measuring the branch counts rather than assuming them, and records n_onscreen alongside n_with_history so that a projection bug and a gate bug stay distinguishable, and so a future degeneracy fails instead of passing.

Mutation-testing the checkers#

Not optional, and it has caught real gaps every time it has been run.

port/racket/test/mutations.sh            # every mutation
port/racket/test/mutations.sh geometry   # only ones whose label matches

The harness copies the port, breaks it one edit at a time, and confirms the suite notices — reading raco test's exit code, never its output, because an earlier harness decided a mutation was caught by grepping for "failed" where raco test prints "failures", and eight real mutations read as passes. It also refuses to proceed if a mutation's pattern did not match exactly once, so an edit that silently changed nothing is an error rather than a spurious success.

Seventy mutations are caught. Six are documented as not caught, with reasons — emitted_radiance's sidedness branch is unreachable once intersect_scene face-forwards the normal, bsdf_eval's dispatch is dead until there is a second material, vlerp has no caller in the ported surface, the w == 0 half of absorb's guard is provably a no-op, focus_distance's compensated sums give bit-identical answers to a naive fold on every scene that exists here, and the per-frame camera never reaches anything downstream that reads it. Recording what a suite cannot see is worth as much as recording what it can.

energy-acceptance.rkt and history-roll.rkt are the two suites the harness does not run per mutation, and the reason is worth stating rather than burying: they render eight and ten warmed configurations respectively, plus references — ~25s and ~40s, against ~1.6s for every other suite combined — which would take the harness from four minutes to over an hour. They also would not catch anything. Every row either checks is a mean over frames that frameloop-conformance has already compared pixel by pixel, so a mutation that moves a number there moves one there first; empirically, with both excluded the harness still misses nothing. They are acceptance statements about the estimator, not discriminators between a correct port and a broken one. MUTATIONS_FULL=1 includes them.

Step 8's forms were mutation-tested too, and the first run found a hole in one of them. define-rng-layout verifies that a declared alias like (gi-cand-dim 0 U) = (bsdf-dim 0 U) holds — the alias that makes ReSTIR GI at M=1 comparable to the two-bounce path tracer per sample. It compared dimension indices and never checked that the two blocks were in the same bounce, so moving gi-cand to another stream satisfied the check perfectly while destroying what it protects: hash4 folds the bounce into the key, so equal indices in different streams are different draws. A checker that validates the easy half of a contract is worth about as much as no checker, and only breaking it on purpose says which half you wrote.

Both fixtures passed on their first run, and mutation testing found real gaps in each. A fixture generated from an implementation tests the branches that implementation happens to take, and that set is smaller than it looks:

  • EPS is unreachable through any render. Every ray starts at the camera or at a point already offset by shadow_epsilon, which is ten times larger.
  • Every quad in every bundled scene is a rectangle, so the off-diagonal dot(u, v) terms of the quad solve vanish identically.
  • The two acceptance gates agree trivially on near neighbours. A few pixels apart on a flat surface, plane distance and depth difference are both ~0, so swapping one gate for the other was a measured no-op. Only distant partners on the same plane separate them.
  • A one-ulp defect sits under any libm tolerance. Reverting the compensated sum in the balance heuristic's denominator was invisible to REL-TOL = 1e-12, which is why both suites now assert exactness separately from the tolerance.

The first two are covered by calling the intersection routines directly, the third by widening the gate partners, the fourth by the exactness assertion.

As a regression test#

Useful independently of any port. The dimension layout is a contract between pt/core/rng.py and pt/kernels/_common.py, and a silent change to either produces two "independent" draws that are the same number — a failure that looks exactly like plausible noise. This turns it into a diff.

verify_conformance.py is mutation-tested: a flipped hash value, a perturbed uniform, a moved alias, a renumbered bounce id and a changed shade_bounce(0) are each caught.

Regenerating must produce a byte-identical file. Everything here is a pure function of the source, so a diff in rng_vectors.json means the RNG contract moved — a breaking change for the kernels, the twin tests and every cached reference in .ref_cache/ — and a diff in geometry.json means an epsilon, an intersection routine or the direct-lighting estimator moved. Both are worth noticing on a git diff rather than discovering later. That property is the Python-side regression test; there is no separate verifier for the geometry fixture because there is nothing it could check that regeneration does not.
