Findings worth keeping

Forty things that were measured rather than assumed — including the hypotheses that were eliminated, which are worth as much as the answers.

Source: ARCHITECTURE.mdunderstanding-oriented

Things that were measured rather than assumed, and that took real work to get right. Each is documented at the code that depends on it.

fixed — a real defect, found and corrected
open — measured, unexplained, still true of the code today
method — no defect: a lesson about how to measure, or a trap avoided
non-finding — a negative result worth keeping — dead code that should stay, a latent defect held shut, or a check that passed
StatusTag40 findings

A shadow-ray epsilon that cancels itself#

fixed

Offsetting the ray origin forward by eps and shortening the far end by eps makes the two cancel, putting the light's own surface exactly on the t < limit boundary — a comparison then decided by rounding, which f64 and f32 resolve differently. It caused 31% of pixels to disagree between the reference and the kernel. Shortening by twice the offset, with a scene-scale-relative term, took that to 0.2%.

documented at core/geometry.shadow_epsilon

ti.template() does not thread a scalar counter#

fixed

The natural stateful RNG — rand(seed) advancing a caller-local counter — fails silently in Taichi: every draw in a kernel returns the same number, and the output still looks like plausible noise. The fix was the design the architecture already called for: fully stateless, explicitly-indexed draws.

documented at kernels/_common.py

A seed that never reached the RNG#

fixed

The scene's :seed flowed through the whole IR and was never mixed into the hash, so two "independent" reference renders were byte-identical and the measured noise floor was exactly zero.

documented at core/rng.hash4

Bias detection needs both estimators' variances#

method

E[MSE(X, Y)] = vx + vy + bias². Comparing against the reference's noise floor alone charges the estimator's own variance to bias — uniform MC at 400 spp against a uniform reference at 4000 spp reports as badly biased, though it is the reference's estimator.

documented at validation/bias.bias_report

The sign test assumes independent pixels; reuse violates that#

method

ReSTIR's neighbouring pixels literally share samples, so a per-pixel binomial test is wildly over-confident and flags every configuration, including provably-unbiased ones. Subsampling beyond the reuse radius fixes it — but too large a stride leaves no statistical power, so sign_test_n is always reported.

Which statistic you measure decides whether you see the effect#

method

For the variance ordering (uniform > RIS > ReSTIR), the mean per-pixel variance is dominated by a handful of fireflies and moved under 5% across estimators; the median samples pixels that were never noisy, where RIS genuinely loses. At the 90th percentile — where perceptible noise lives — the expected ordering is clean: RIS 1.9× better than uniform, ReSTIR 1.5× better than RIS.

ReSTIR must be measured per frame, not accumulated#

method

Consecutive frames are strongly correlated through the reservoirs, so averaging N of them is worth far fewer than N independent samples. Comparing accumulated means makes ReSTIR look worse than plain RIS.

documented at Engine.reset_accumulation

"Many lights" is not a light count#

method

Sixteen lamps in a ceiling grid look like the regime resampling is for, but every floor point sees them at similar distance and angle, their target values differ by under 2×, and RIS beat uniform by ~2%. Scattering the same sixteen through the volume at different heights, sizes and intensities is what creates the condition.

documented at scenes/many_lights.edn

Where 1/M reuse actually goes wrong — and why it took a purpose-built scene#

method

The intuitive place to look for spatial-reuse bias is a geometry discontinuity. It is the wrong place: the geometric similarity gate rejects those neighbours, so it is already doing, crudely, the job MIS does properly. On an ordinary Cornell box :naive and :mis differed by 0.2% — under the noise floor.

The bias needs neighbours that pass the gate — same surface, same normal, same depth — yet disagree about which samples are possible. Lights standing perpendicular to a flat floor produce exactly that: cos_light flips sign along a razor-sharp line across a plane, so two adjacent pixels are geometrically identical while one assigns zero probability to samples the other can produce. On scenes/bias_demo.edn, :naive converges to 60% of the correct energy; :mis and :pairwise-mis land within 3%.

The detection sweep is the clearest statement of what bias is:

sppnaive excessmis excessnaive energy vs truth
250+1.71x−0.16x0.603
600+4.39x−0.03x0.596
1200+5.74x−0.32x0.594
2000+5.64x−0.33x0.597

The energy deficit is constant; only the noise hiding it shrinks. And the biased estimator is 15x less noisy than the correct one while being 12x further from the truth — clean, stable, and wrong. Tonemapped, the two renders are hard to tell apart, which is the whole argument for measuring instead of looking.

Bias also shows in the convergence curve's shape, sooner than in its MSE#

method

slope_verdict fits the tail of the curve: an unbiased estimator keeps falling near -1, a biased one flattens onto its bias-squared plateau. It needs only the curve to have turned over, not full convergence, so it fires earlier than the excess-over-noise test. Fitting the whole curve instead of the tail dilutes the evidence, because early checkpoints fall steeply either way.

A scene can be pathological in a way that breaks the statistics#

method

The first version of bias_demo used small, intense emitters. Uniform sampling's error went heavy-tailed, a handful of fireflies dominated the MSE, one realization landed 15x above its own expectation, and the harness reported uniform MC as biased against a reference uniform MC had produced — with a convergence slope of +0.71, error rising with sample count. Nothing was biased; a statistic was being applied to a distribution it does not describe. Dialling brightness down kept the support-splitting effect and made every estimator well behaved.

Temporal reuse is unbiased under every mode without reprojection — and that is informative#

method

With no reprojection the canonical and temporal streams share a shading point and therefore a target function, so the balance heuristic collapses exactly to the 1/M form: measured, the three weightings agree to f32 rounding. The :bias toggle genuinely does nothing to that pass. It says the bias in reuse comes from combining different domains — which is why it lives in the spatial pass, and why it returns the moment reprojection is turned on.

documented at kernels/restir.py

Reprojecting a static scene correctly gives back the same shading point, so the bias has to come from somewhere else — and where it comes from is magnification#

method

This was the surprise. wi and dist are re-derived exactly from the stored world-space point, and the target function is view-independent for a Lambertian surface, so a correctly reprojected pixel lands on the point it started from. A large lateral camera slide changes nothing measurable. The residual error is the pixel quantization: hits_prev[floor(projection)] is whatever that pixel's jittered ray hit, up to one previous-frame footprint away along the surface. That footprint is measured in the previous frame's resolution on that surface, so it is only large when the surface was smaller on screen then — i.e. under magnification. Dolly the history camera back by k and the reprojected point moves k current footprints, which is spatial reuse at radius k arriving through the temporal pass with no MIS weight to correct it. A moving camera has no single reference, unless the camera comes back#

method

Per-frame comparison against a converged reference would need one reference per pose, which is the expensive part of the harness multiplied by the frame count. Alternating between two poses and measuring only the frames at pose A gives one reference that serves every measured frame, while every measured frame's history came from pose B and was genuinely reprojected. Accumulating across the path is not an option: the mean of images taken from different viewpoints is not an image of anything, so frames are extracted individually by differencing the accumulator (Engine.render_sample_radiance).

A firefly in the scene geometry cost the MSE test all of its power#

method

The first draft of reproject_demo inherited bias_demo's layout, in which four light posts run through the back wall — putting wall points at distance ~0 from a samplable light point, where the geometric term goes as 1/d². Single-pixel radiances reached 5000 against a scene mean near 1, the mean squared error ran 25000x the median squared error, and it did not fall with more frames, it wandered. The bias detector read +0.14x excess on an estimator losing a tenth of the scene's light. Moving the posts inside the box dropped MSE by 37x, and the same test then read +2.57x and fired. The lesson is not that MSE is the wrong statistic — it is that MSE is the fragile one, and it fails by quietly returning "unbiased". Total energy, which uses the whole image as its sample, resolved the deficit either way.

documented at scenes/reproject_demo.edn

The temporal gate cannot be the spatial gate#

fixed

accept_neighbor compares hit.t, the distance from the eye. Two pixels of one frame share an eye, so that is a fair proxy for depth; two frames of a moving camera do not. Dolly by 20% and a perfectly reprojected static point — same position, same normal — fails the test, and all reuse is discarded exactly when it would have paid. The camera-independent test is the plane distance, |dot(p_prev − p_q, n_q)| < 0.02·t, which tolerates the tangential slop a floored pixel index guarantees while still catching a disocclusion.

documented at core/integrators/di_restir.accept_reprojection

A stream that found nothing still has confidence M#

fixed

Found by the reference-vs-kernel diff, and only findable that way: combine_streams skipped streams whose sample was None including their M, so on pixels where the canonical stream drew M candidates and found every one unusable, the reference's W was off from the kernel's by a clean factor of two. The selected sample matched, so nothing but a numeric comparison could see it — and under the 1/M rule that discarded M is precisely what should have been diluting the other stream.

documented at core/reservoir.combine_streams

Clearing the accumulator must not rewind the RNG#

fixed

During a reprojecting camera drag the accumulated image is cleared every frame. Rewinding the sample counter with it would hand every frame of the drag the same uniforms — the same sub-pixel jitter and, because sample_light_point(li, u, v) is deterministic in u and v, literally the same M points on the same M lights. The canonical stream would stop contributing new information the instant the drag began, which is the opposite of what preserving reuse across the drag is for. Engine.spp is therefore the accumulated count and ctx.spp_index is the RNG counter; they coincide everywhere except that one path.

A per-sample reference-vs-kernel diff proves transcription, not measure#

method

It is the strongest test in the project and it has a blind spot worth naming: the two implementations are supposed to compute the same thing, so a wrong pdf, a double-converted Jacobian or a doubled cosine makes them agree exactly as well as being right does. For direct lighting the closed form covered that gap. One bounce further there is no closed form, so the indirect term is anchored against a deterministic quadrature in a different parameterization — which never forms a pdf at all, and so cannot share the mistake. Measured: the Monte Carlo estimator and the quadrature agree to 1.6% or better at three shading points, one of them on a sphere.

documented at validation/indirect.py

Differencing beats comparing when the new term is a small fraction of the image#

method

Two bounces are only ~13% brighter than one on the Cornell box, so a completely broken indirect term still lands inside the 2%-of-pixels tolerance a whole-image reference-vs-kernel comparison can afford. The fix is not a tighter tolerance: it is to make the indirect term the entire signal by subtracting the one-bounce image, which the RNG layout makes exact rather than statistical — vertex 0 keeps its stream, so the direct term cancels instead of being averaged away. The same trick converges the indirect anchor comparison in 8000 samples instead of millions. Another instance of the recurring lesson that which statistic you measure decides whether you can see the effect at all.

"A stream that found nothing still has confidence M" has a second half, and missing it cost 25%#

fixed

The rule was already known and already implemented inside combine_streams. What was still wrong is the step before: the code that decides which neighbours become streams filtered them on having a sample. Dropping an empty neighbour hands the canonical stream a weight of 1 on the taps that came up empty and m_c < 1 on the taps that did not — the canonical gets counted twice, and the estimator runs bright in proportion to the number of neighbours.

It hid in direct lighting for a simple reason: a reservoir of 8 or 32 light candidates is almost never empty. A path reservoir at M = 1 is empty whenever the bounce ray escapes or its vertex is black, which on the Cornell box is about 70% of pixels. Measured on the indirect term against a converged reference:

spatial reuseK=1K=3K=5
dropping empty neighbours1.0861.2011.284
counting their confidence1.0021.0061.005

Three things about how it was found are worth keeping. The total image energy read 0.9953 — perfectly healthy — because the indirect term is 11% of that scene; differencing against the direct-lighting render was what made the signal visible, which is the same lesson the two-bounce work already recorded, now in its expensive form. The per-pixel probes were useless: a single pixel's estimate is heavy-tailed enough that a DI control showed the same "bias" on a near-black pixel, and the diagnosis only became possible aggregated over the whole frame. And the decisive measurement was not the image at all but E[w_sum], the estimator's own internal quantity, which must be preserved by a reuse pass and can be read straight out of the reservoir buffers.

The same rule inverted for the temporal pass, and that asymmetry is real#

fixed

A spatial neighbour is a fresh reservoir over candidates drawn this frame, so its confidence belongs in the denominator whether or not it found something. The history is this pixel's own accumulated state, and an empty one carries an M that was already spent diluting earlier frames; feeding it back dilutes the canonical again every frame with nothing to replace it. Measured, treating the two the same way took the indirect term from 0.993 to 0.939.

The measurement stands and the asymmetry is still what the code does. The explanation above is not the whole story: the next finding shows the dominant effect in that number is duplicated samples, not spent confidence, and that E[M] is not what distinguishes the healthy configuration from the broken one.

A sample that travels through spatial reuse and comes back is counted twice#

fixed

The one defect where each reuse mechanism was individually within ~1% of the converged reference and the two together read 0.955, with the deficit growing in m_cap — 1/2/5/20/60 giving 0.985/0.981/0.973/0.955/0.939. Three explanations were measured and eliminated before the real one, and the eliminations are worth as much as the answer:

  • Not a warm-up transient. Flat across 512 frames; block 64 read 0.958 and block 480 read 0.948. There is no recovery to wait for.
  • Not the randomness of M. The obvious repair — always admit the history as a stream so the confidence recursion stops depending on whether a reservoir found anything — made it worse, 0.959 to 0.943, and degraded temporal-only reuse from 0.9975 to 0.9896.
  • Not confidence accounting at all, which is the surprise, because M really is being asked to mean two things (fresh candidate count, and accumulated confidence over a chain of merges). Separating the two counters cannot fix this, and the decisive measurement below is why.

The mechanism is that temporal reuse makes a sample persist and spatial reuse spreads it, so the two together let one draw reach several pixels that then feed each other. Measured on cornell_restir_gi, the spatial pass's input buffer held only 31% distinct reconnection vertices — 3.2 copies of the average sample — where either pass alone leaves it 100% distinct. The balance heuristic adds one denominator term per stream, so a draw held by k streams has its probability counted k times, every m_i comes out too small, and the estimator loses energy. It is invisible in either pass alone because neither can produce a merge whose streams hold the same draw.

The decisive measurement is a change that holds every confidence fixed and moves only sample identity: roll the pre-spatial reservoir into the history instead of the post-spatial one. m_cap, the radius and E[M] are untouched — 46.5 against 46.8 at m_cap 20 — and duplication drops to exactly 1.00:

m_cap1252060
history from post-spatial0.9850.9810.9730.9550.939

copies per sample1.351.481.833.246.27

The m_cap scaling vanishes entirely, which is the point: m_cap was never the cause, only the knob that set how long a sample survived to be copied. The residual 0.990 is the spatial-only deficit, a separate and much smaller matter. Direct lighting keeps the post-spatial roll — the mechanism is not payload-specific, but every number in the DI tables was measured under it.

One test that looked decisive and is not, recorded so it is not repeated: sweeping the spatial radius at fixed m_cap does not track duplication — radius 5 has the most copies (3.88) and the smallest deficit (0.969). A small radius also means near-identical shading points, so it moves two things at once.

documented at Engine._make_advance · core/render._render_restir

Measuring a reuse estimator from a cold start hides its steady-state bias, and the dilution is bigger than the effect#

method

The natural protocol — reset, render N frames, compare the accumulated mean — is fine for a path tracer and wrong for ReSTIR, for a reason that has nothing to do with the correlation between frames that reset_accumulation already exists to handle. A ReSTIR run begins as something close to a path tracer and becomes the estimator under test over roughly m_cap frames, so an accumulation started at frame 0 averages two different estimators together. Measured on cornell_restir_gi at 96px, sweeping the number of warm-up frames discarded before a 200-spp accumulation:

warm-up frames082464128256
mis1.00370.99660.99190.98730.98720.9870
naive1.00100.99390.98920.98410.98420.9834

The reading settles by 64 frames and does not move again through 256. From cold it reads 1.004 — above the truth, and indistinguishable from the uniform control at 1.0009 ± 0.0004. The whole 1.3% deficit is invisible under the protocol that looks most obviously correct. --warmup defaults to 64 for this reason, and the same discard is what makes the [reuse] breakdown readable.

The control that keeps this honest is the no-reuse row, measured with the same warm-up: both passes off at M = 1 is the two-bounce path tracer sample for sample, and it reads 1.0004 ± 0.0027. So the discard is not itself creating the deficit.

documented at validation/gi.render_indirect · tests/test_estimators.py::TestRestirGiStatistics

Discarding warm-up frames is not the same as rewinding the sample counter, and the reference twin could only do the first#

fixed

reset_accumulation() clears the image, keeps the reservoirs and sets spp_index back to 0, so the measured frames draw uniforms 0 … spp-1 however long the warm-up was — which is exactly what one_bounce_image renders, and therefore what makes the direct term cancel per sample rather than averaging away over spp. The engine has always done this. pt/core/render.py did not: it took spp and nothing else, so the only warm-up it could express was slicing frames off the front, which leaves the measured frames on samples warmup … warmup+spp-1 against a subtrahend on 0 … spp-1.

The residue is small and lands in the worst possible place. Measured on gi_bias_demo at 24px, the no-reuse row — which is the uniform control sample for sample — read 0.9989 against a control of 1.0099, 0.011 apart, on a statistic whose entire job is to resolve a few percent. With the rewind the two agree to 3e-4, which is the eps/d reconnection residual and nothing else.

Found by the Racket port, which needed the reference to reproduce a measurement only the kernels could previously make. The general form is worth more than the fix: "every estimator lives twice" has to include the protocol the estimator is measured under, not just the estimator — and a twin that cannot describe the experiment will not be noticed as long as nobody asks it to run one.

documented at pt/core/render._render_restir · warmup=

Reuse loses energy that MIS weighting does not explain — open, and now measurable#

open

With the duplicated-sample fix in place and warm-up discarded, path reuse is still short of the converged indirect term, and the amount is a property of how far reuse reaches rather than of the weighting. On gi_bias_demo, whose twelve panels put a support boundary inside every reuse radius:

uniformno reusetemporal onlyspatial onlyboth
mis0.99961.00000.97400.92000.8960
naive—1.00000.97400.85210.8463

and on cornell_restir_gi the same shape an order of magnitude smaller — 1.0009 / 1.0009 / 0.9950 / 0.9926 / 0.9863. Four things this pins down:

  • The estimator is exact without reuse, warmed, on both scenes. Whatever this is, it enters through a reuse pass.
  • Temporal-only is not weighting-sensitive — mis and naive agree to four decimals, exactly as the direct-lighting finding above says they must without reprojection — and it is still 2.6% short. So this is not the 1/M defect.
  • MIS does real work and is not sufficient. It recovers 0.06 of the 0.16 that 1/M loses on the spatial pass, and leaves 0.08.
  • The deficit scales with the spatial radius, at fixed resolution, m_cap and tap count: at 128px, radius 4/8/16/32 gives mis 0.9529/0.9287/0.9002/0.8960.

Two hypotheses are already eliminated. It is not a resolution artifact of a 32px radius on a small image — 64/128/256px give 0.898/0.896/0.910 with the radius held fixed. And it is not the measurement: the uniform control reads 1.0006 ± 0.0002 at 256px and the no-reuse identity reads 1.0000.

One thing worth noticing before diagnosing: at a small radius the ordering inverts — at radius 4, naive reads 0.985 and mis 0.953. A weighting that is supposed to add back energy leaving less of it than the biased one is the shape of an over-large MIS denominator, which is the same family as the two defects already found (a stream counted that could not have produced the sample; a sample counted twice). That is a lead, not a conclusion.

Whole-image energy would have shown none of this. The same runs read 0.9803 (mis) and 0.9692 (naive) on total energy where the indirect term reads 0.8984 and 0.8386 — and the MSE-excess verdict, the harness's primary bias detector, returns "unbiased (within noise)" for every row, because bias² on 18% of an image never clears both estimators' variance at a few hundred spp. On this class of defect the energy ratio resolves at tens of sigma what the MSE test cannot see at all, which is why pt validate puts it in the first column and prints the MSE verdict beside it.

documented at validation/gi.py · pt validate gi_bias_demo

A metric worth having is worth anchoring, and the anchor found the bug#

fixed

FLIP was implemented here from the paper, and the paper's own constants left it 7% high on every achromatic difference. What found that was not review: it was running NVlabs' flip-evaluator once in a throwaway environment and diffing against it — the same move as analytic.py re-deriving its own vector math, one level up.

The bug was in cmax, the distance the colour difference is normalized by. The natural derivation is the largest HyAB distance the sRGB gamut admits, green to blue, which comes to 308.12; the reference behaves as though it were 203.29. The value is now measured rather than derived, and measuring it took two constraints that pin it with no freedom left: on the neutral axis the response is 0.0575405 * d^0.7 across d in [1.7, 53] to six digits, which fixes gpt/(gpc*cmax), and the upper branch of the piecewise redistribution then reproduces the reference at d = 80 to six digits as well. Fitting a curve to a black-box oracle is a poor way to obtain a constant and a very good way to discover that yours is wrong.

What the anchor establishes, and what it does not:

referencehere
identical images0.0000000.000000
one-pixel edge shift, achromatic0.0313680.031369
flat colour shift, pure chroma0.27820.3966
Cornell render, 8 spp vs converged0.10430.1136

The achromatic path is exact — spatial CSF filter, edge-clamped padding, both feature detectors, CIELab, HyAB along L, redistribution — and a residual sits in the two chromatic channels, reading high. Per-channel DC gains were fitted as an explanation and rejected (best residual still 20% of the distances, correlation 0.86), so it is structural and not yet found. On this renderer's images that is about +0.01 of mean FLIP with the ordering between estimators preserved, which is what a column that ranks estimators needs and is not enough to quote against a published number. tests/test_flip.py pins the exact half to 1e-4 and the chromatic half to the measured gap, so either improving or worsening the agreement fails a test rather than silently changing what the column means.

An epsilon that keeps a Jacobian finite must not reach the target function#

fixed

reconnect decides two things at once — whether a shift exists, and whether the target is non-zero — and a grazing-cosine threshold added for the first silently does the second. Refusing a shift is unbiased: the stream drops out of the MIS denominator as well as the sum, exactly as a geometric-gate rejection does. But the same threshold applied to p_hat throws away the canonical stream's own sample, the one the pixel actually traced, and nothing else covers it. A target that is zero where the contribution is not is biased, full stop. Found by the per-sample identity against the uniform path tracer and by nothing else — the image was fine, and every invariant test on the Jacobian still passed. Sidedness is now tested strictly, with no epsilon, and the Jacobian guards only against arithmetic with no answer. A grazing reconnection is a large finite number, which is the correct answer and a genuine firefly.

A bounce ray's epsilon and a shadow ray's solve different problems and must not share a scale#

fixed

Reusing shadow_epsilon for the scattered ray looked like economy. A shadow ray's margin is scaled by its own length, because what it has to survive is a near-cancellation at its far endpoint; a bounce ray has no far endpoint yet, so the same formula scales it by the primary ray's length — ~1300 units in the Cornell box — and puts the origin 0.13 units off the surface. A bounce landing in a corner 0.37 units away then starts a third of the way along its own path. That is invisible in an image, and it is fatal to reuse, because the pdf belongs to the sampled direction while reconnection re-derives the direction from the stored vertex: the two stop being the same ray. Measured on the worst sample of the ReSTIR-GI-equals-path-tracer identity, it was 169%.

The fix is a separate constant, and its value was measured rather than argued — the two failure modes turn out not to overlap:

The residual at 1e-5 is not tolerated on a threshold but explained: the disagreement is eps / d, distant vertices agree to 1e-3 unconditionally, and the outliers' size is predicted by their own eps / d to within a factor of ~1.2.

Reciprocity does not pin a Jacobian's direction#

method

J(a->b) * J(b->a) == 1 is the natural invariant to reach for, it catches a transposed term, and it holds just as well if the whole ratio is inverted — so it cannot tell a correct shift mapping from one that is upside down, which is precisely the error worth catching. The identity case has the same blind spot. What closes it is arithmetic done by hand for an asymmetric configuration: a vertex twice as close must give J = 4, not 1/4. Both numbers are asserted, on both implementations, rather than one being derived from the other.

documented at tests/test_core.py::TestReconnection

A read-modify-write hazard, removed rather than documented#

fixed

Spatial reuse reading and writing reservoir_curr in place is a race the moment the pass runs in parallel: each pixel sees an arbitrary mixture of updated and stale neighbours. Reading reservoir_temporal and writing reservoir_curr costs one buffer and eliminates it.

documented at graph/builder.py

Nine scenes and 55,000 compared values still had two blind spots, and only mutation testing found them#

method

The step-3 conformance fixture compares the Racket port against the Python per ray and per sample across four scenes, and passed on the first run. Deliberately breaking the port then showed two defects it could not see at all:

  • EPS is unreachable through a render. Every ray a scene traces starts either at the camera, far from everything, or at a shading point already offset by shadow_epsilon — which is 1e-3 at minimum, an order of magnitude above EPS = 1e-4. So the guard that stops a ray re-hitting the surface it left never fires, and deleting it changes no pixel of any scene. It still has to be right, because bounce rays are what it exists for.
  • Every quad in every bundled scene is a rectangle. intersect_quad solves for (s, r) against a 2×2 Gram matrix whose off-diagonal is dot(u, v), and with rectangles that is exactly zero, so the two terms carrying it vanish identically. Corrupting one of them is a no-op on all nine scenes. Even a sheared quad mostly hides it: the corrupted s has to land outside [0, 1] while the correct one is inside, or the wrong solve still returns the same t.

Both are now covered by calling the intersection routines directly with hand-chosen rays, rather than by hoping a scene reaches them. The general form is worth keeping: a fixture generated from the implementation tests the branches that implementation happens to take, and that set is smaller than it looks.

documented at docs/architecture/conformance/ · port/racket/test/mutations.sh

emitted_radiance's sidedness test is dead code, and should stay#

non-finding

intersect_scene face-forwards the hit normal against the incoming ray, so dot(n, wo) = dot(n, -dir) >= 0 for every hit any caller can construct — and every caller in both implementations gets its hit from the intersector. The dot(n, wo) <= 0 branch that suppresses emission from a one-sided lamp's back face is therefore reachable only at exact tangency; over the coverage scene's emitter hits the smallest dot(n, wo) is 0.050. Inverting the test changes no pixel. It is left in place on both sides because the two implementations must read the same, and because a future intersector that stops face-forwarding would need it immediately.

PAIRWISE_MIS is not unconditionally a partition of unity, and what keeps that harmless is an invariant nothing states#

non-finding

The rule weights each non-canonical stream against the canonical one only:

m_i(y) = (1/k) · a_i / (a_i + b), m_c(y) = (1/k) · Σ_i b / (a_i + b)

Summing gives (1/k) · Σ_i (a_i + b)/(a_i + b) = 1 — but only over the i where a_i + b > 0. A pair with a_i + b = 0 is 0/0, is skipped, and contributes to neither weight, so the total comes out k_valid / k. That needs the canonical stream to be blind to y and at least one neighbour to be too, which is unreachable on a scene where the canonical sees everything its neighbours see — and that is exactly the configuration tests/test_core.py::TestMisWeights uses (four streams on one wall, one light), which is why it has always passed. Measured over the samples the estimator actually weights, at 32×32:

The balance heuristic is 0% on all three: it renormalizes over the whole denominator, so a stream that cannot produce y drops out and the rest take up the slack.

It changes no pixel, and not by luck. b is M_c · p̂(streams[0].hit, y), and every call site passes the canonical stream's own hit as the merge target — so b = 0 exactly when combine_streams' own factor p̂(hit_q, y) is 0, and w = m_i · p̂_q · W_i · J is zero whatever m_i was. Of the 39 and 6 shortfall samples above, zero had a nonzero contribution at the merge point. The latent defect is held shut by streams[0].hit == hit_q, which is true everywhere and enforced nowhere. Reconnection is where the temptation to break it arrives, since it is about evaluating a sample at a shading point it was not drawn at.

Two of the render graph's five compile stages are dead code on every shipped scene#

non-finding

Measured across all nine bundled scenes and four configuration variants: nothing is ever culled, and nothing is ever aliased — n_physical equals the resource count in all thirteen. Culling finds nothing because every AOV and every persistent resource is kept alive by default_keep_alive and the graph declares no pass whose output nobody reads; aliasing finds nothing because the remaining transients have distinct descriptors, so every bucket holds one resource. The topological sort is likewise the identity on all thirteen, because the builder declares passes in dependency order already, so its tie-break decides nothing.

That is not an argument for deleting them — a graph is a substrate for graphs that do not exist yet, and assemble_di_graph is one client. It is an argument for testing them on graphs built for the purpose. Four synthetic graphs now do: a dead pass, an aliasing chain, a diamond declared sink-first, and a persistent resource read at v0. A fifth exists solely because first-fit and last-fit slot assignment agree unless two slots are free at once — with one free slot the rule is untested however many aliasing graphs there are.

The general lesson is the one steps 3 and 4 recorded, in its sharpest form so far: a fixture generated from an implementation tests the branches that implementation takes, and on a render graph with one client that can be less than half the code.

describe() is a stronger acceptance test than it looks#

method

It prints slot numbers, so requiring it character-for-character transitively pins declaration order, the topological tie-break, culling, the lifetime intervals and the greedy colouring's first-fit rule — five things with no numeric output of their own. A port that gets any of them subtly different produces a plan that behaves identically and prints differently, which is exactly the divergence worth catching: it means the two are no longer the same schedule, and every later claim about buffer reuse is about a different graph.

documented at graph/plan.describe

"ReSTIR GI at M=1 with reuse off is the two-bounce path tracer" was written in five places and is not true#

fixed

What gi_cand_dim's alias buys is that the two draw the same numbers, so they can be compared per sample rather than statistically. Their outputs still differ: the bounce ray leaves from x1 + eps·n, so the vertex it finds is not exactly on the ray from x1 — the path tracer evaluates the integrand at the direction it sampled, reuse re-derives the direction from the stored vertex, and the two differ by about eps / d. Measured at 20×20 on cornell_restir_gi: 103 of 185 samples agree bit for bit, one exceeds 1e-3, the worst is 7.3e-3, and the totals agree to 2.7e-5.

tests/test_core.py::TestGiRestirIdentity states it correctly and asserts the residual's shape — most samples exact, few above 1e-3, sums within 1e-3 — rather than a tolerance that would hide a structural error in the tail. The docstrings and the porting spec did not, and the wording invites exactly the equality assertion that then fails with no explanation attached. All five now say what the alias actually buys.

CPython's sum() over floats is not a left fold#

fixed

Since 3.12 it uses Neumaier compensated summation, so sum([0.1]*10) is exactly 1.0 where folding left gives 0.9999999999999999. core/reservoir.py uses it in two places that matter — the balance heuristic's denominator and NAIVE's Σ M_j — and a reimplementation that folds left disagrees in the last place. The disagreement appears only with three or more live streams, because compensated and naive summation coincide for two, which means it is invisible in the temporal merge and shows up only under spatial reuse. It cost an afternoon to find precisely because a one-ulp difference sits under any tolerance loose enough to allow for libm.

The accuracy is wanted on its own terms, independently of which language provides it: the denominator is Σ_j M_j p̂_j(y) over streams whose targets can span orders of magnitude — the entire premise of many_lights — so the naive fold's error is largest exactly where resampling matters most. The port implements Neumaier explicitly rather than inheriting it.

documented at port/racket/vecmath.neumaier-sum

A tolerance that is never used should still be asserted at zero#

method

Both conformance suites allow a 1e-12 relative tolerance for libm and then separately require every compared value to be exactly equal, because the two claims are different and only the second one catches a one-ulp defect. Reverting the compensated sum above is invisible to the tolerance and immediately visible to the exactness check. The split also keeps the failure legible: if the exactness check is the only one failing, the port is fine and the platform's libm differs.

Two independent implementations of the same f64 arithmetic agreed bit for bit, across 55,184 values#

non-finding

The Racket port's acceptance check allows a 1e-12 relative tolerance for the three libm calls in the path — tan in the camera basis, sqrt in every length, sin/cos in the sphere light. Measured, the tolerance was never used: every compared value, including radiance accumulated through a shadow ray and a geometry term, was exactly equal. The tolerance stays, because a different platform libm is a real possibility and the alternative is a suite that fails for a reason it cannot explain; but the useful fact is that "same operations in the same order" is a stronger guarantee in practice than it sounds, and a port that needs a loose tolerance to pass has a structural difference, not a rounding one.
