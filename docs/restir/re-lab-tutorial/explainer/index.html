The problem

Clean, beautiful, and wrong

ReSTIR's failure mode is that it does not look like a failure. Fold a neighbour's reservoir in with the wrong weight and the image gets less noisy while converging to the wrong answer. Both renders below are the same scene at the same sample count. One of them is missing 40% of the light in the room.

A biased estimator that is also noisy gets caught by everyone. A biased estimator that is clean is the one that ships.

Three minutes of background

What reuse is, and where it goes wrong

If ReSTIR is already familiar, skip to the next act. This is the shortest path from resampled importance sampling to the exact place the bias enters.

A reservoir is an O(1) summary of a stream

Rather than keeping M candidates and picking one at the end, keep one sample and a running weight. Chao's rule: on each arrival, replace the held sample with probability w / w_sum. The survivor ends up distributed proportional to the target function p̂, and the reservoir never grows.

The accept test is the whole algorithm. Everything after this is about combining reservoirs that were not built at the same shading point.

Reuse, and the weight that has to change

Your neighbour — or this pixel one frame ago — already drew M candidates and kept a good one. Borrowing it is where the 10–100× variance reduction comes from. But that sample was selected as good for them, and you are about to evaluate it at your pixel, where the geometry, normal and BSDF differ.

This is the distinction the entire codebase is organized around:

  • 1/M is the correct MIS weight for combining M candidates drawn i.i.d. from one distribution at one shading point. Initial RIS uses it and is unbiased.
  • 1/M is the wrong weight for combining reservoirs whose target functions differ. That is reuse, and it needs a real mᵢ(y) accounting for how likely each contributing stream was to have produced that sample, evaluated with its own target.

Every valid rule has to be a partition of unity — Σᵢ mᵢ(y) = 1 for any y in the combined support. Three rules satisfy it. Drag the target ratio and watch what separates them:

Interactive · MIS weights Two streams merging one sample y
At a ratio of exactly 1.00 the two streams share a target function and the balance heuristic collapses algebraically to Mᵢ/ΣMⱼ — the three rules become the same number. Measured in the codebase, they agree to f32 rounding. With exactly two streams, pairwise-mis reduces to the balance heuristic term for term.
Why that matters more than it looks

Temporal reuse without reprojection reuses this pixel's own history at the same shading point, so the ratio is pinned at 1.00 and the bias toggle is provably inert. The bias in reuse does not come from reuse — it comes from combining different domains. Which is why it lives in the spatial pass, and why it returns the instant reprojection is switched on.

Push the ratio toward zero and the difference becomes stark. A sample the neighbour could never have produced still gets charged that neighbour's full share under 1/M, while the balance heuristic correctly sends its weight to zero. That gap is the 40% of missing light in the first figure.

The design

So write the estimator as data

This renderer was specified as a compiler before a line of it existed. The founding brief asked for "an EDN or Clojure s-expression DSL dialect that encodes a high-level symbolic intent IR, and then we lower that… all the way down to the optimized code," and three messages later pinned the contract: enforce referential transparency and determinism at pass boundaries.

That is an unusual commitment for a renderer, and everything in the last act is a consequence of it.

The scene is already s-expressions

Not a config format that resembles Lisp — actual homoiconic data, with macros that expand before analysis, and a reader that keeps a source span on every node so diagnostics can point a caret at the offending form.

;; pt/scenes/cornell.edn — the whole scene is one form (scene {:integrator {:type :direct-lighting :light-sampling :uniform :spp 256 :max-bounces 1} ;; (cornell-box) expands to five walls plus the luminaire; ;; (concat ...) splices our own geometry alongside it. :geometry (concat (cornell-box) [{:id :s_left :prim :sphere :center [185 120 169] :radius 120} {:id :s_right :prim :sphere :center [368 90 351] :radius 90}]) ;; Area lights BIND to a geometry id, so the emitter you can hit ;; and the one you sample for NEE can never disagree. :lights [{:id :area0 :type :area :geometry :light}]})

Seven pure transforms lower that to executable kernels, and every intermediate is a first-class artifact you can dump and diff. Step through them:

A pass is a declaration, not a function call

Each pass states what it reads, what it writes, and what invalidates it. Because that is data, the compiler can order the graph, cull unreachable work, compute resource lifetimes, alias buffers — and reject at build time any pass that reaches for a buffer it never declared.

;; the shape of a pass declaration (define-pass ReSTIR.GI.Spatial ;; reads one buffer, writes another — never both, see below #:reads [hits reservoir_temporal] #:writes [reservoir_curr] #:dirty [camera scene]) ; deliberately NOT sppSpatial reuse reads one buffer and writes another because reading and writing reservoir_curr in place is a race the moment the pass runs in parallel — each pixel would see an arbitrary mixture of updated and stale neighbours. One extra buffer removes the hazard rather than documenting it.

The propagation is then mechanical — change something, and the compiler works out what has to re-run. Two mechanisms compose here, and it is worth watching them disagree: a pass is seeded dirty by its declared domains, and then dirtiness flows forward along data edges. The reuse passes deliberately omit spp, yet advancing the sample still re-runs them — because hits is rewritten every sample and they read it.

The reuse algebra has exactly one copy, and two inhabitants

ReSTIR DI resamples points on lights; ReSTIR GI resamples path suffixes through a reconnection vertex. Those are different payloads, and it would be natural to write the combination logic twice. The subtlest bugs in this project have all lived in that algebra, so it exists once, parameterized by a pair of functions.

;; all the payload-dependence there is (define-reuse-domain di #:target phat-at ; (scene hit y mode) -> ℝ≥0 #:jacobian (λ _ 1.0)) ; ← not a simplification. see below (define-reuse-domain gi #:target gi-phat-at #:jacobian reconnection-jacobian) ; |dω_q / dω_r|, both cosines at x2 ;; and the combination, written once, over either of them (define (combine-streams scene q streams us #:domain dom #:bias b) (define p̂ (domain-target dom)) (define J (domain-jacobian dom)) (define m (mis-weights scene streams b dom)) (finalize (for/fold ([r (empty-reservoir)]) ([s streams] [mi m] [u us]) (define y (stream-sample s)) (if (not y) ;; found nothing — but its confidence still counts (absorb r #f 0.0 (stream-M s) 0.0) (absorb r y (* mi (p̂ scene q y) (stream-W s) (J scene y (stream-hit s) q)) (stream-M s) u))) (if (eq? b 'naive) 'ris 'mis)))
The detail worth stopping on

The DI Jacobian is identically 1 not because the payload is a world-space point — the GI payload is a world-space point too. It is 1 because a light sample's pdf is stored in area measure, which is view-independent, while a path sample's pdf is in solid angle at the vertex it was traced from. The measure the density lives in is what decides whether a shift needs a Jacobian, and storing that as a per-domain hook rather than an if is what let a test inject a deliberately wrong Jacobian and watch the answer move.

Every estimator lives twice

There is a pure-f64 reference in pt/core/ and a Taichi kernel in pt/kernels/, and they are held to agreeing per sample — not statistically. That is only possible because no draw is stateful: every random number is a pure hash of (pixel, sample, bounce, dim, seed) and ti.random() is banned outright.

Three things fall out of that, and the third is what made the last act possible:

  • The reference and the kernel draw the same numbers, so they can be diffed per sample rather than by convergence.
  • Clicking a pixel re-derives the path that was actually traced, so the inspector shows evidence rather than a plausible re-roll.
  • Vertex 0 keeps its stream at any depth, so a two-bounce render draws exactly what a one-bounce render draws at its first vertex — and subtracting the two cancels the direct term exactly, instead of averaging it away.

That last one is not a convenience. The indirect term is about 11% of the Cornell box, so a completely broken second bounce still lands inside the tolerance a whole-image comparison can afford. Differencing makes the indirect term the entire signal.

The payoff

Four bugs that no render would have shown

Each of these is a two-line change to the estimator. In every case the image stayed fine and a number moved a great deal. They are ordered by how much they had to be hunted.

1  A stream that found nothing still has confidence M

The rule was known and implemented inside the combination. What was still wrong sat one step earlier: the code deciding which neighbours become streams filtered them on having a sample. Drop an empty neighbour and the canonical stream gets counted twice — so the estimator runs bright in proportion to the neighbour count.

It hid in direct lighting for a mundane reason: a reservoir of 8 or 32 light candidates is almost never empty. A path reservoir at M=1 is empty whenever the bounce ray escapes or its vertex is black, which on the Cornell box is about 70% of pixels.

Total image energy read a perfectly healthy 0.9953 throughout, because the indirect term is 11% of that scene. Per-pixel probes were useless — a single pixel's estimate is heavy-tailed enough that a direct-lighting control showed the same fake bias. The decisive quantity was not the image at all but E[w_sum], read straight out of the reservoir buffers.

2  A sample that travels through spatial reuse comes back and is counted twice

This is the one that took real work. Each reuse mechanism alone sat within about 1% of the converged reference. Together they read 0.955 — and got worse as m_cap rose, which no explanation involving either pass alone could account for.

Temporal reuse makes a sample persist; spatial reuse spreads it. Together, one draw reaches several pixels that then feed each other. The balance heuristic adds one denominator term per stream, so a draw held by k streams has its probability counted k times, every mᵢ comes out too small, and the estimator quietly loses energy.

Two scales, two charts — never two y-axes on one plot. The decisive measurement holds every confidence fixed and moves only sample identity: E[M] is 46.5 against 46.8 at m_cap 20, and duplication drops to exactly 1.00. The m_cap scaling vanishes entirely, which is the point — m_cap was never the cause, only the knob setting how long a sample survived to be copied.

Three explanations were measured and eliminated before the real one. The eliminations are worth keeping, because each is a plausible story that a reasonable person would spend a day on:

✕A warm-up transient. Flat across 512 frames — block 64 read 0.958, block 480 read 0.948. There is no recovery to wait for.
✕The randomness of M. The obvious repair — always admit the history as a stream — made it worse, 0.959 to 0.943, and degraded temporal-only reuse from 0.9975 to 0.9896.
✕Confidence accounting at all. The surprise, since M really is being asked to mean two things. Separating the counters cannot fix it — the decisive measurement holds E[M] fixed.
✕And one test that looked decisive and is not. Sweeping the spatial radius does not track duplication: radius 5 has the most copies (3.88) and the smallest deficit (0.969). A small radius also means near-identical shading points, so it moves two things at once.

3  Measuring from a cold start hides the whole thing

The natural protocol — reset, render N frames, compare the accumulated mean — is right for a path tracer and wrong for ReSTIR, for a reason unrelated to the frame-to-frame correlation that is already handled. A ReSTIR run begins as something close to a path tracer and becomes the estimator under test over roughly m_cap frames. Accumulate from frame 0 and you are averaging two different estimators together.

From cold it reads 1.004 — above the truth, and indistinguishable from the control. The entire 1.3% deficit is invisible under the protocol that looks most obviously correct. The reading settles by 64 frames and does not move again through 256, which is why --warmup defaults there.

4  A bounce ray's epsilon and a shadow ray's must not share a scale

Reusing shadow_epsilon for the scattered ray looked like economy. A shadow ray's margin is scaled by its own length, because what it has to survive is a near-cancellation at its far endpoint. A bounce ray has no far endpoint yet, so the same formula scales it by the primary ray's length — about 1300 units in the Cornell box — putting the origin 0.13 units off the surface. A bounce landing in a corner 0.37 units away then starts a third of the way along its own path.

Invisible in an image. Fatal to reuse, because the pdf belongs to the sampled direction while reconnection re-derives the direction from the stored vertex — the two stop being the same ray. On the worst sample of the ReSTIR-GI-equals-path-tracer identity it was 169% off.

The two failure modes turn out not to overlap, so the constant was measured rather than argued.
SCATTER_RELidentity error (worst)ref-vs-kernel disagreementmean ratio

1e-5 is 300× better on near-field error and costs nothing at all in reference-vs-kernel agreement. One step further and f32's own error in hit.p stops being negligible against the offset, so the two implementations start disagreeing about which surface a bounce found. The residual is not tolerated on a threshold but explained: the disagreement is eps/d, and the outliers' size is predicted by their own eps/d to within a factor of about 1.2.

The recurring lesson

In every one of these, the statistic decided whether the defect was visible at all. MSE against a reference returned "unbiased (within noise)" on estimators losing a sixth of the indirect term, because bias² on 11–18% of an image never clears both estimators' variance at a few hundred spp. The energy ratio resolves the same defect at tens of sigma. Low variance is not correctness, and neither is a passing test on the wrong quantity.

Unresolved

And one that is still open open

With the duplicated-sample fix in place and warm-up discarded, path reuse is still short of the converged indirect term, and the amount is a property of how far reuse reaches rather than of the weighting.

On cornell_restir_gi the same shape appears an order of magnitude smaller: 1.0009 / 1.0009 / 0.9950 / 0.9926 / 0.9863.

Four things this pins down:

  • The estimator is exact without reuse, warmed, on both scenes. Whatever this is, it enters through a reuse pass.
  • Temporal-only is not weighting-sensitive — mis and naive agree to four decimals, exactly as the direct-lighting result says they must without reprojection — and it is still 2.6% short. So this is not the 1/M defect.
  • MIS does real work and is not sufficient. It recovers 0.06 of the 0.16 that 1/M loses on the spatial pass, and leaves 0.08.
  • The deficit scales with the spatial radius, at fixed resolution, m_cap and tap count.
Indirect energy vs spatial radius gi_bias_demo @128px, mis
Two hypotheses are already eliminated. Not a resolution artifact — 64/128/256px give 0.898/0.896/0.910 with the radius held fixed. And not the measurement — the uniform control reads 1.0006 ± 0.0002 at 256px and the no-reuse identity reads 1.0000.
The lead, stated as a lead

At a small radius the ordering inverts. At radius 4, naive reads 0.985 against mis at 0.953. A weighting that is supposed to add energy back leaving less of it than the biased one is the shape of an over-large MIS denominator — the same family as the two defects already found: a stream counted that could not have produced the sample, and a sample counted twice.

That is a lead, not a conclusion. It is where the next session starts.

Where it goes

#lang restir/estimator

The design brief called for a Python EDN library. The shipped reader is 283 hand-written lines — because a stock reader gives you no spans, and spans are what make a 23-code error catalog able to point a caret at the offending form.

That is the whole argument for the port in one detail. Racket's syntax/parse hands you, for free, precisely the thing this design had to rebuild by hand: source locations threaded through macro expansion, structured error reporting, and a macro stepper that shows the lowering happening.

What becomes writable

Today the reuse domains, the three MIS rules, the RNG dimension layout and the pass structure are hardcoded. They are all already data in shape — they are just data written in Python. Making them a language means the last act's findings stop being commit diffs and become programs you can hold side by side:

;; finding 2, as a diff in a language where the estimator is the program (define-frame-loop restir-gi #:passes (initial+temporal spatial resolve) - #:history reservoir_curr) ; a sample can travel and return + #:history reservoir_temporal) ; duplication forced to 1.00Everything else — m_cap, the radius, E[M] — is held fixed. Only sample identity moves, which is exactly what makes the measurement decisive.

And the unbiasedness obligation currently lives in a docstring and a parametrized test. As a judgment it could be stated once and randomly tested against every rule anyone writes:

;; the proof obligation every weighting rule owes (define-judgment-form partition-of-unity #:contract (⊢ rule streams y) [------------------------------------------- (⊢ rule streams y) (side-condition (≈ 1.0 (Σ (mᵢ rule streams y))))])Note that the estimator uses mᵢ(yᵢ) — each stream weighted at its own sample — and those do not sum to one, nor do they need to. The obligation is over a fixed y, which is exactly the kind of quantifier distinction a judgment form makes unambiguous and a docstring does not.

The Taichi kernels stay in Python, and so does the converged-reference oracle. The port is of the layer where the thinking happens — the front end, the graph, and the pure reference that is the denotational spec — into a substrate where the estimator is something you write, and the compiler can check.
