Measure a change for bias

The protocol that turns “the tests pass” into a correctness claim.

Source: docs/how-to/measure-a-change-for-bias.mdtask-oriented

You changed the estimator. Green tests are not a correctness claim and neither is a render that looks right — a biased ReSTIR produces a clean, beautiful, wrong image. This is the procedure that turns "it works" into a number.

Work through it in order. The early steps are seconds and catch most mistakes; the later ones are minutes and catch the rest.

1. Change both twins#

Every estimator lives twice — a pure-f64 reference in pt/core/ and a Taichi kernel in pt/kernels/ — and they are held to agreeing per sample, not statistically.

concernreferencekernel
reservoir algebra, MIS weightscore/reservoir.pyinlined in kernels/restir*.py
ReSTIR DI passescore/integrators/di_restir.pykernels/restir.py
ReSTIR GI passescore/integrators/gi_restir.pykernels/restir_gi.py
frame loop, history rollovercore/render._render_restirEngine._make_advance + graph/builder.py

If you added a random draw, you must also allocate a dimension in both core/rng.py and kernels/_common.py, or two "independent" draws silently become the same number — which looks exactly like plausible noise. See Add a draw.

2. Run the twin tests#

The fastest useful signal in the repository:

.venv/bin/python -m pytest tests/test_kernels.py::TestRestirGiKernels -q
.venv/bin/python -m pytest tests/test_core.py -k reconnection -q

They compare the reference and the kernel per pixel under all three weightings — including naive, precisely because it is the wrong estimator. The two implementations must agree about what the wrong answer is, or a later bias measurement would be measuring a kernel bug.

Know this test's blind spot. The two implementations are supposed to compute the same thing, so a wrong pdf, a double-converted Jacobian or a doubled cosine satisfies the diff exactly as well as being right does. It proves transcription, not measure. That is what steps 3 and 4 are for.

3. Get a verdict#

.venv/bin/pt validate <scene> --spp 400

Read three sections:

  • [bias] — excess error over both estimators' noise. E[MSE] = var(est) + var(ref) + bias², so comparing against the reference's noise floor alone charges the estimator's own variance to bias and reports honest estimators as broken.
  • [convergence] — an unbiased curve keeps falling near -1; a biased one flattens onto its bias-squared plateau. This fires earlier than the excess test, because it needs only the curve to have turned over.
  • [variance] — measured per frame, never accumulated. Consecutive ReSTIR frames are correlated through the reservoirs, so averaging N of them is worth far fewer than N independent samples, and comparing accumulated means makes ReSTIR look worse than plain RIS.

Converged references are cached by scene fingerprint in .ref_cache/, so a repeat run is cheap — see validation/reference_cache.py.

4. If the change touches GI, measure the indirect term#

Whole-image statistics cannot see a GI estimator. The indirect term is about 11% of a Cornell box, so a defect in the only quantity ReSTIR GI is responsible for arrives at a whole-image test divided by nine. A 25% spatial-reuse bias, once measured, read 0.9953 on total energy.

.venv/bin/pt validate cornell_restir_gi --res 96 96 --spp 200 --ref-spp 1200
.venv/bin/pt validate gi_bias_demo      --res 128 128

Four rules govern this measurement. Violating any of them produces a confident wrong answer.

Difference against a one-bounce render. Use validation/gi.render_indirect and indirect_verdict rather than rolling the subtraction by hand. Vertex 0 keeps its RNG stream, so the direct term cancels exactly rather than averaging away. It is valid only for :uniform and :restir-gi, which share vertex 0's draws; require_shared_direct_term refuses the rest rather than returning a number nobody can interpret.

Discard 64 warm-up frames. A ReSTIR run becomes the estimator under test over roughly m_cap frames, so an accumulation started at frame 0 averages in the path tracer it began as. Measured, that dilution turns 0.987 into 1.004 and hides the deficit completely. The reading settles by 64 frames and does not move through 256.

Warm the no-reuse control identically. Both passes off at M = 1 is the two-bounce path tracer sample for sample; it must read 1.000. That is what keeps the discard honest — otherwise the discard itself is a suspect.

Read energy before MSE. On a term this small, bias² never clears both estimators' variances, so the MSE-excess verdict stays inside its margin for an estimator losing a sixth of the indirect term. The energy ratio over the whole frame resolves the same defect at tens of sigma. pt validate prints energy in the first column and the MSE verdict beside it for exactly this reason.

5. Do not reach for these#

Per-pixel probes. A single pixel's estimate is heavy-tailed enough to show fake bias on a direct-lighting control. Aggregate over the frame.

FLIP. validation/flip.py ranks and localizes; it does not detect bias. A converged biased estimator is perceptually smooth and scores well. Use it for "which of these looks closest" and "where do they differ" — see Compare two renders perceptually.

A render. Tone-mapped, the correct and the 40%-dark images of bias_demo are hard to tell apart, and the biased one is 15× less noisy.

6. Write down what you measured#

When a measurement settles something, add it to ARCHITECTURE.md's Findings worth keeping and reference it from the code that depends on it.

Record the hypotheses you eliminated. They are worth as much as the answer — one finding there records three eliminations and a non-finding, and every one of them is a test somebody else now does not have to repeat.
