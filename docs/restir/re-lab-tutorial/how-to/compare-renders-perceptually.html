Compare two renders perceptually

Run FLIP, read the map, and know the three questions it cannot answer.

Source: docs/how-to/compare-renders-perceptually.mdtask-oriented

Every other statistic in this harness is a number about radiance. FLIP answers a different question: would a person see the difference, and where.

Compare two images#

.venv/bin/pt flip out/reference_linear.npy out/restir_linear.npy -o out/flip.png

Both inputs are the linear .npy buffers, not the PNGs — pt render writes <name>_linear.npy beside every image for this reason. The output is a magma error map and a mean FLIP score on stdout.

Get a map per estimator during validation#

.venv/bin/pt validate cornell_restir_gi --flip-maps out/flipmaps

pt validate reports mean FLIP per estimator in its own column and writes one map per estimator into the directory you name.

In the GUI there is a FLIP vs reference view that lights up once a converged reference is in the cache.

Read the map#

Bright regions are where a viewer would notice a difference; dark regions are where they would not. FLIP is bounded per pixel, which is the property that makes it useful here: unlike MSE — which a single firefly has been measured to own at 25,000× the median — nothing can dominate it. A map that is uniformly dim with one bright patch is telling you the truth about where your change landed.

Know what it will not tell you#

It is not a bias detector. A converged biased estimator is perceptually smooth and scores well. This is not a limitation to work around; it is the reason the energy and MSE sections of pt validate still carry the verdicts. If your question is "is this correct", go to Measure a change for bias instead.

Do not quote the number against a published one. This implementation is independent and anchored against NVlabs' flip-evaluator. The achromatic path is exact — 0.031368 against 0.031369 on a one-pixel edge shift, through the spatial CSF filter, both feature detectors, CIELab, HyAB and the redistribution. A residual sits in the two chromatic channels and reads high: about +0.01 of mean FLIP on this renderer's images, with the ordering between estimators preserved. That is what a column ranking estimators needs and is not enough to cite.

tests/test_flip.py pins the exact half to 1e-4 and the chromatic half to the measured gap, so either improving or worsening the agreement fails a test rather than silently changing what the column means.

Why the anchor exists#

Implementing FLIP from the paper left it 7% high on every achromatic difference. What found that was not review — it was running NVlabs' evaluator once in a throwaway environment and diffing against it.

The bug was in cmax, the distance the colour difference is normalized by. The natural derivation is the largest HyAB distance the sRGB gamut admits, green to blue, which comes to 308.12; the reference behaves as though it were 203.29. The value is now measured rather than derived.

The general form is the same move validation/analytic.py makes one level down by re-implementing its own vector math: an anchor that shares code — or a derivation — with the thing it validates can be corrupted by the same mistake and agree anyway.
