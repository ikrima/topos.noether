Two Reservoirs, Two Promises

learner · practitioner · contributorauthoredcurrent

Two reservoirs appear in re-lab. Each retains one winner, an accumulated weight, a represented candidate count, and a final normalization. Seen only as data structures, they look like duplicated implementations waiting to be generalized.

They are not.

A direct reservoir selects a point on an emitter. Once selection is complete, its contract is deliberately narrow: test visibility from the current surface to that point exactly once, then evaluate the contribution. NEE, RIS, and ReSTIR DI can make candidate selection progressively richer without changing that promise.

A path reservoir selects something larger: one contribution from a multi-bounce transport history. Its winner must identify not only where light was found, but how the contribution can be reproduced, which technique and material events generated it, and whether it can be mapped into another pixel’s domain. Reuse may require replay, reconnection, inverse- support evaluation, and a non-identity Jacobian.

The containers resemble each other because both use weighted reservoir sampling. Their contracts remain separate because they are evidence about different random variables.

One surface, two questions

Section titled “One surface, two questions”

Consider one camera ray landing at the primary surface point x. The renderer splits its lighting work there.

The direct branch asks:

Which point y on an emitter should illuminate x now?

Regardless of whether y was proposed by light sampling or by the BSDF, re-lab projects it into the established emitter-area domain. The selected LightSample carries the point, emitter identity, normal, emission, and proposal densities needed for the direct target. Visibility is deliberately deferred until after selection, so direct evaluate pays for one shadow ray—not one ray per proposal.

The indirect branch asks a different question:

Which transported contribution z explains light arriving at x through a multi-bounce history?

A PathContribution is one canonical term from the traced contribution tree. It carries exact RGB radiance and a scalar target, but also its technique, path depth, random identity, selected material events, source domain, origin surface, and the compact lineage needed to reproduce or reconnect it. That extra state is not debugging decoration. It defines what the sample means when another pixel tries to reuse it.

The same sampling silhouette

Section titled “The same sampling silhouette”

Both branches stream weighted candidates through the same conceptual summary:

  • Y is the retained winner.
  • p_hat(Y) is its non-negative scalar target value.
  • w_sum accumulates candidate weights.
  • M counts the source candidates represented, including reused aggregates.
  • W normalizes the selected winner so it represents that accumulated population.

This is why the two reservoir types rhyme. The shared shape captures the algebra of weighted selection: retain constant-size state while representing many candidates.

It does not say what Y represents, which measure its target lives in, how it can move between pixels, or what work remains before it becomes radiance. Those are domain promises, not WRS bookkeeping.

The winners promise different things

Section titled “The winners promise different things”
Question Direct reservoir Path reservoir
What is selected? A LightSample on an emitter A PathContribution with reproduction lineage
Where does it live? The emitter-area sample domain A source and evaluation path domain
What remains after selection? One deferred visibility query Possibly shift, replay, reconnection, support, and visibility work
How is it reused? Re-evaluate the same light point at another compatible surface Map a retained contribution into a new target domain
What can invalidate reuse? Reprojection, surface compatibility, or direct support Domain, topology, material event, inverse support, reconnection, or visibility failure
What happens to measure? The established identity area mapping has J = 1 General hybrid mappings can require a factored, non-identity Jacobian

The most important row is the third. A finalized direct reservoir is ready for the one visibility query promised by direct evaluate. A retained path contribution may first need to establish that an equivalent target contribution exists at all. A forward shift can fail, its inverse can lack support, or its probability measure can change.

Those failures are estimator evidence. Treating them as exceptional plumbing—or omitting them because the resulting image looks plausible—would erase part of the correctness argument.

Why a universal reservoir would mislead

Section titled “Why a universal reservoir would mislead”

A generic Reservoir<T> could be a reasonable internal helper for elementary WRS arithmetic. It is not a sufficient semantic interface for the renderer.

If T is a LightSample, evaluate(scene, hit, reservoir) promises exactly one deferred shadow ray. Adding RIS or spatiotemporal direct reuse changes how candidates reach the reservoir; it does not weaken that final evaluation contract.

If T is a PathContribution, the renderer must answer additional questions before it can use the winner at a new target:

  • Can the source contribution be reproduced from its retained identity?
  • Does a valid shift or reconnection exist in the target domain?
  • Could each inverse source technique have produced the shifted contribution?
  • Which target values and Jacobian convert the source density correctly?
  • How many replay, reconnection, and visibility rays did that decision actually cost?

Putting both winners behind one broad evaluate or combine operation would make these obligations implicit. Separate Reservoir and PathReservoir types let the direct branch keep its narrow propose/evaluate invariant while the indirect branch exposes explicit trace, resample, shift, and evaluate stages.

The design therefore shares mathematics without pretending that shared mathematics erases meaning.

One progression, two estimator families

Section titled “One progression, two estimator families”

The estimator ladder is easier to understand as two related progressions:

Direct-light family What changes Path-contribution family What changes
NEE Select one fresh light point PT Trace multi-bounce contributions directly
RIS Compress many fresh light points Path RIS Compress the fresh contribution tree
ReSTIR DI Reuse compatible light-point reservoirs GRIS Introduce explicit mappings between contribution domains
— — ReSTIR GI Reconnect supported diffuse suffixes
— — ReSTIR PT Replay and reconnect general-material paths

RIS is the conceptual bridge. Both families learn to represent many candidates with one winner. Reuse then diverges because a light point and a transported path contribution do not have the same support, mapping, or evaluation costs.

This is also why comparing only M or the final candidate count is misleading. Direct reuse deliberately retains one final shadow ray. Exact path reuse discloses fresh trace, replay, reconnection, and visibility rays separately. Equal candidate counts are not necessarily equal work budgets.

The branches compose as radiance

Section titled “The branches compose as radiance”

The selected objects never need to become interchangeable. Their independently evaluated results do:

That late composition protects both sides. The completed direct estimator stays regression-pinned while the indirect branch grows from PT through general path reuse. Replay can explain the light point selected by one branch and the path lineage selected by the other without pretending they are the same kind of evidence. Accelerated backends can lower each domain into suitable SoA storage without changing its logical meaning.

When the Inspector shows a reservoir, ask four questions:

  1. What random variable does its winner represent?
  2. In which probability measure was it selected?
  3. What work remains before it becomes radiance?
  4. What evidence proves that it can be reused here?

Those questions are more durable than memorizing either data structure.

To see the two branches in motion, compare the direct-resampling tutorial with the path-reuse tutorial. Then continue to bias, support, and Jacobians for the change-of-variables argument, or open the sampling-domain chapter in the interactive atlas.
