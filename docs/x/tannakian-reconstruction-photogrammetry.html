02 / CORRESPONDENCE

Before views can agree, we must decide what is the same.

Two photographs are not yet a reconstruction. First comes the quiet, treacherous problem of identity across appearances: which mark in one image is the same world-point as which mark in another?

1Observe

Each camera receives a flattened appearance. Depth has disappeared.

2Correspond

The red marks are proposed identities across views. Without this step, the images are merely a pile.

3Triangulate

Each matched image point defines a ray in space. Their intersection proposes a hidden 3D point.

As the match drifts, the rays cease to tell a single geometric story. This is reconstruction failure before optimization even begins.

Correspondence is the bridge between appearance and coherence.
03 / COHERENCE

The views must admit one common world.

A correct match is not just “visually plausible.” It must survive the harder test: can all the sightings be explained as projections of one latent object?

This is why relations between appearances carry reconstructive information. The images alone do not say which fragments belong together; the compatibility structure does.

04 / GAUGE

The same photographs can describe many coordinate worlds.

Reconstruction rarely returns “the object” in an absolute sense. It returns an equivalence class: a world modulo transformations that leave the observations unchanged.

Rotate the entire scene and all cameras together. Translate them together. Rescale a monocular reconstruction and compensate the camera translations.

The pixel evidence does not care.

What is reconstructed is often not X, but [X] = X / gauge.

06 / FAILURE MODES

Reconstruction fails in only a few fundamental ways.

Photogrammetry makes the abstract failure taxonomy visible: blindness, missing coverage, incoherence, and instability.

Blindness

Different worlds, same appearances.

The probe family does not separate candidates. The forward map has a nontrivial null space.

Incomplete coverage

Some structure never enters the measurements.

An unseen surface cannot be recovered from image evidence alone. Priors may fill the gap, but that is inference beyond observation.

Incoherence

The views refuse to tell one story.

Wrong correspondences or incompatible transformations cannot be explained by one latent world.

Instability

Tiny image errors, huge world errors.

Poor camera geometry or an ill-conditioned inverse amplifies noise during reconstruction.

We had “observe” and “cohere.” Photogrammetry inserts the difficult middle operation: establish identity across appearances.

That is the deepest thing photogrammetry contributes to the Rosetta Stone. It turns reconstruction from a clean theorem into an operational struggle with finite evidence.

Yoneda asks for all probes. Tannaka asks for coherent realizations. Gelfand asks for observables. Descent asks how local pieces glue. Inverse problems ask whether causes can be inferred from effects.

Photogrammetry asks:

What happens when the probes are few, the appearances are noisy, the correspondences are unknown, the reconstruction is gauge-redundant, and the world still has to be recovered?

Photogrammetry is not the eighth theorem. It is the laboratory where reconstruction theory becomes real.
