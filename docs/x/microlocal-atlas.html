The decisive lift

From a place to a place-and-direction.

Ordinary analysis marks the points where smoothness fails. Microlocal analysis lifts every such point into its cotangent fiber, where the missing directional information becomes visible.

The resulting geometry is shared by apparently different theories. A distribution carries a wavefront set. A differential equation carries a characteristic variety. A sheaf carries a microsupport. A principal symbol generates a Hamiltonian flow. They all meet inside the same phase space.

01

A point becomes an entire sky of directions.

The delta distribution is concentrated at one base point, but its localized Fourier transform never decays in any direction. The singular support is a point; the wavefront set is the punctured cotangent fiber above it.

That distinction is the first lesson of the atlas: location alone is too coarse. A point defect can contain every high-frequency orientation at once.

Atlas reading

In phase space, the point at x = 0 rises into a vertical conic line of nonzero covectors.

02

An edge lifts to its conormal bundle.

A jump across a hypersurface is smooth along the surface and singular across it. The correct microlocal object is therefore not the tangent direction of the edge, but its normal covector.

At every boundary point, the atlas draws the two nonzero rays ±dφ. The whole family is the conormal bundle N* S \ 0.

Atlas reading

Move the probe along the red interface: the base point changes, while the normal fiber direction remains locked.

03

A phase function writes a Lagrangian graph.

For a rapidly oscillating family a(x)e^{iλφ(x)}, the local covector is the phase gradient dφ. As the wavelength shrinks, energy concentrates on the graph of this one-form.

This is the geometric-optics seed of Fourier integral operators: phase carries geometry; amplitude rides on top of it.

Semiclassical lens

The finite-frequency function is smooth. The displayed set is its semiclassical wavefront as λ → ∞.

04

Ellipticity removes hiding places.

The symbol of an elliptic operator never vanishes away from the zero section. There are no nonzero characteristic directions in which a singularity can evade inversion.

The microscope panel shows high frequencies attenuated by the resolvent. Roughness in the solution must already be present in the forcing.

Regularity

For 1 − Δ, the multiplier (1 + |ξ|²)⁻¹ suppresses fine-scale oscillation.

05

The symbol becomes a dynamical law.

For the wave operator, the characteristic set is a cone. Its principal symbol is also a Hamiltonian, and its Hamiltonian vector field traces the bicharacteristics along which singularities propagate.

The same curve appears twice: in phase space as symplectic flow, and after projection as a physical light ray or wavefront trajectory.

Propagation

Scrub evolution or let the animation run. The red singular fronts stay attached to the blue characteristic flow.

06

Sheaves detect one-sided failure.

A sheaf replaces Fourier decay by a propagation test across level sets. Its microsupport records the covectors for which local sections cannot be continued through a boundary.

Standard and costandard extensions can select opposite conormal signs. Microlocal sheaf theory therefore resolves a causal asymmetry that an unsigned edge picture cannot see.

Directional logic

Toggle standard and costandard in the microscope panel. The allowed green conormal flips orientation.
